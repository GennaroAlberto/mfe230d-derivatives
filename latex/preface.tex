\chapter*{About these notes}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{About these notes}

These are lecture notes for MFE 230D, \emph{Derivatives: Quantitative Methods}, in the
Master of Financial Engineering program at UC Berkeley Haas: analytical pricing beyond the
Black--Scholes formula, Monte Carlo methods and their Greeks, path-dependent and
multi-asset payoffs, and the numerical methods behind them.

They grew out of the discussion sessions I taught for the course, which is taught by
Dmitry Livdan and builds on material developed by Eric Reiner. The text, the proofs, the
demos and the wording of the exercises were written for this repository; any mistakes
are mine.

\paragraph{How to use them.} Each chapter has three parts. The notes, in this document,
develop the theory with complete proofs wherever the argument teaches something, and
with precise references where it does not. The exercises are collected in a separate
document; solutions are not published, so that the course is not spoiled for the next
class. The interactive demos, marked \textcolor{cobalt}{$\blacktriangleright$}, are Python
notebooks that open in Google Colab in one click.

\paragraph{Prerequisites.} Stochastic calculus for finance at the level of a first
course: Brownian motion, It\^o's formula, risk-neutral pricing and the Black--Scholes model.
The companion notes for IEOR 222 cover all of it. Python with NumPy and SciPy.

\paragraph{Corrections.} Please report errors, typos and unclear passages as issues on
the \href{\repourl/issues}{GitHub repository}.
